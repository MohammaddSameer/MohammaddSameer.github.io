Ministry of Public and Business Service Delivery and Procurement - Work Term Report Summer 2026

Welcome to my website! My name is Mohammad Sameer and I'm a 4th year Software Engineering student at the University of Guelph. This blog will shed light on my 4 month co-op work term as a Junior Technical Analyst at the Ministry of Public and Business Service Delivery and Procurement (MPBSDP).

About the Ministry of Public and Business Service Delivery and Procurement

The Ministry of Public and Business Service Delivery and Procurement (MPBSDP) is one of many ministries under the Ontario Public Service (OPS). They're responsible for providing many of the digital, administrative, and procurement services that support the Ontario government and the public. The ministry works to improve how government services are delivered by modernizing technology systems, managing data securely, and supporting efficient operations across different ministries and agencies. Its work focuses on making government services more accessible, reliable, and efficient for both employees and residents throughout Ontario.

The Labour and Transportation Cluster (LTC) is a cluster within the ministry that provides IT and digital support for the Ministry of Transportation (MTO) and the Ministry of Labour, Immigration, Training and Skills Development (MLITSD). LTC supports a wide range of technology-related services, including data analytics, application support, cybersecurity, and process automation. Within LTC, the Digital Solutions Branch (DSB) works closely with different teams and stakeholders to develop and maintain tools that help improve daily operations and decision-making.

Fun Facts

  • The Ontario Public Service (OPS) employs over 60,000 people across the province in areas such as technology, transportation, healthcare, finance, and public administration.
  • The Ministry of Transportation (MTO) oversees more than 16,000 kilometres of provincial highways and over 2,800 bridges across Ontario.
  • Reporting Structure of my team: OPS -> MPBSDP -> LTC -> DSB -> IMO

Job Description - Junior Technical Analyst Co-op

As a Junior Technical Analyst within the Labour and Transportation Cluster (LTC), I was apart of the Information Management Office (IMO) where I supported digital solutions and internal business processes for clients and stakeholders. My role involved using Microsoft Power Platform tools such as Power BI, Power Apps, Power Automate, and SharePoint to develop and maintain dashboards, automate workflows, manage data and content, and support day-to-day operations. I was apart of the MTO portfolio on my team so I also worked closely with MTO clients to gather requirements, provide updates, troubleshoot issues, and ensure solutions met business needs. Through this experience, I gained a better understanding of how technology is used within government to improve efficiency and support large-scale operations.

One of the most interesting aspects of the position was the opportunity to work directly with clients while also developing technical solutions that had a real impact on their daily work. I enjoyed creating Power Automate workflows and Power Apps solutions that reduced repetitive manual tasks and improved processes for users. Building interactive Power BI dashboards was also rewarding because it allowed me to transform data into meaningful visual insights that supported decision-making. The position required both technical and interpersonal skills, including problem-solving, communication, teamwork, and experience with Microsoft Power Platform tools. While some foundational knowledge came from university courses in programming and systems analysis, many of the practical skills were developed through hands-on experience during the co-op term. Working in a professional and client-focused environment helped me strengthen both my technical abilities and my confidence communicating with stakeholders and adapting to business requirements.

Goals

Develop Proficiency in Power Apps

One of my main goals was to get comfortable using Power Apps since it was a big part of my role. I spent time going through Microsoft Learn, looking at apps my team had already built, and trying to create my own. As the term went on, I got hands-on experience working with real apps, connecting them to SharePoint, and making improvements. This helped me understand how low-code tools are actually used in a work setting, which will be useful for future roles. I feel like I built a strong foundation, but there's still more to learn when it comes to more advanced features.

Build foundational skills in Microsoft Power BI

I also wanted to improve my Power BI skills since I was working with data and reporting. I focused on learning how to clean and transform data using Power Query, and then turn that data into useful dashboards with charts, KPIs, and filters. Over time, I worked with real datasets and built dashboards that were used to support decision-making, which made the experience more practical and rewarding. I became more confident not just in using the tool, but also in thinking about how to present data clearly. This is a skill I know I'll keep using in the future as I pursue roles in data science/data analysis.

Develop Strong Communication and Collaboration Skills

Another goal was to improve my communication and collaboration skills, especially since I was working with clients in the MTO portfolio. At the beginning, I mostly observed how my team communicated in meetings and emails, and paid attention to how they explained technical concepts to non-technical users. As I became more involved in projects, I started contributing more by asking questions, giving updates, and helping communicate solutions to clients. I also worked on writing clearer emails and documentation related to my tasks. By the end of the term, I felt much more confident speaking in meetings and working with stakeholders, which is a skill that will be valuable in any future job.

Conclusion

Reflecting on my co-op experience with the Ministry of Public and Business Service Delivery and Procurement as a Junior Technical Analyst, I can confidently say that it was a highly rewarding and educational experience. I had the opportunity to work on a variety of projects that allowed me to develop both technical skills and professional competencies. I'm proud of the growth I achieved and the contributions I was able to make. From working with Power BI, Power Apps, SharePoint, and Power Automate to develop dashboards, applications, and automated workflows, I gained hands-on experience building solutions that supported real business needs. Being part of the MTO portfolio also gave me valuable experience working directly with clients and understanding how technology supports large-scale government operations.

This experience helped me grow both technically and professionally, strengthening my skills in data analysis, problem-solving, communication, and collaboration. I became more confident working with stakeholders, translating business needs into technical solutions, and adapting to new tools and environments. Overall, this co-op has given me a strong foundation in both technical and client-facing work, and it has played an important role in shaping my future career goals.
